Information Design Prototypes

For my first area of exploration, I looked at different ways I could display information. I thought this was a good area to explore, because the way I display my information is important, especially for my audience. Something I kept in mind while making my prototypes is that it's crucial to try not to overload the layout. Toddlers are still learning, so it is important to not throw in stuff for them, expecting them to understand. I made 3 different prototypes under this area of exploration and tried to make each one display differently as much as I believe could potentially work as a final prototype. It was good to discover different ways of displaying information rather than being set to one.

Prototype 1: Horizontal Layout

This is my initial prototype. I have placed all the keys in the center, and the information displayed is in one area rather than it being seperated all over the screen. I laid out the keys in the same side as a real piano would be to display a sense of familiarity, like a real piano. The keys itself are spaced out a bit to allow the users to be able to touch on the correct key. The heading and score are big to allow the users to understand the information displayed.

Prototype 2: Vertical Layout

My second prototype is a vertical version of the initial prototype. I still kept the keys in the same layout to remain the sense of familiarity to in real life piano keys. The only thing I changed was the positioning of the other key infomations to the design. This layout was inspired by some online music makers where information (like editors) is on the left side of the screen such as BeepBox. It gives the prototype a music making feel in a way. I thought this layout was worth exploring because music makers come in different layouts.

Prototype 3: Image Layout

For the third prototype, instead of moving the keys into different spots of the screen, I changed the keys to different images of animals. Alongside, the keys are in squares, rather than it being rectangular. I did with animals, since visual imagery is something commonly used in learning tools for toddlers.

Feedback Prototypes

My second area of exploration is feedback. With my randomising game challenge especially, it is crucial to have proper feedback so that users are able to identify they've gotten something right. I think this area of exploration was good to look into for feedback from my peers, to understand what works best for my users.

Prototype 4: Text Feedback

For my initial prototype for this area of exploration specifically, the feedback displayed is through text. I changed the buttons into colours instead of leaving the note names on the buttons to actually make the challenge, challenging. Depending on the users answer to the challenge, the feedback text will change.

Prototype 5: Animation Feedback

The second prototype is animation. The animation changes depending on wether the user got the answer right or wrong.

Prototype 6: Sound Feedback

My final prototype for this area of exploration was sound feedback. I thought this was a good way to explore feedback, as it is good to consider that the users may not be able to understand visual cues yet, so it can be harder to learn. I added a delay in sound to indicate which key was correctly pressed, and which one was wrong.
