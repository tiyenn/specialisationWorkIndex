Information Design Prototypes

The first area I decided to explore was the information design of my instrument. I feel that this is a key factor to take into consideration and is worth exploring, as my target audience, being toddlers, it's crucial to have the information be easy for them to understand. It won't work if the layout doesn't match their level of expertise and understanding, so I look to make sure it's clean and simple.

I tried 3 different approaches for this. I think it is important to be able to understand where my keys best fit on the screen for my users to freely play, explore, and learn.

Prototype 1: Horizontal Layout

This is my initial prototype. I have placed all the keys in the center, and the information displayed is in one area rather than it being seperated all over the screen. I laid out the keys in the same side as a real piano would be to display a sense of familiarity, like a real piano. The keys itself are spaced out a bit to allow the users to be able to touch on the correct key. The heading and score are big to allow the users to understand the information displayed.

Prototype 2: Vertical Layout

My second prototype is a vertical version of the initial prototype. I still kept the keys in the same layout to remain the sense of familiarity to in real life piano keys. The only thing I changed was the positioning of the other key infomations to the design. This layout was inspired by some online music makers where information (like editors) is on the left side of the screen such as BeepBox. It gives the prototype a music making feel in a way. I thought this layout was worth exploring because music makers come in different layouts.

Feedback Prototypes

My second area of exploration is feedback. I think this was a good area to explore, as it can be good to discover what learning way works the best for my target audience. Each prototype I've made for this area of exploration differs in buttons. This is important as we need to still make sure that the instrument is engaging and aesthetically pleasing.

Prototype 1: Colours Button

For my initial prototype I had in mind for the feedback area of exploration was changing the buttons into different colours. This was one of the more basic yet suiting ideas I had in mind for these prototypes as many games for toddlers in general coorporate colours to help with learning and to be engaging.

Prototype 2: Animals Button

For my second prototype, I changed the buttons into animals. This is another way most sites use to engage the young users with learning.

Prototype 3: Shapes Button

Lastly, I turned my buttons into shapes. Same reason as the last two layouts, the use of shapes is also a good visual people use to engage the young audience to learn.
